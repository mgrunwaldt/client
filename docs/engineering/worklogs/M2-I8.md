# M2-I8 Player Client Worklog

## Issue Contract

Implement the player-client half of `mgrunwaldt/match_server#20`. The backend is
authoritative for pitch geometry, player identity and position, the ball, camera
window, contact, receiver control, and every flight point. The client applies
one versioned transform to every visual element, locks the camera for the whole
input/playback/result sequence, and never infers an outcome from what it draws.

## Architecture

- `src/match/field-transform.ts` is the only field-percent to Three.js world
  transform. Pitch, stadium anchors, models, labels, ball, and trajectory use
  the same x/y/z convention and physical `68 x 105 m` coefficients.
- `field-camera.ts` consumes the backend normalized camera window. It does not
  recenter after a result, and the Corner window retains the goal, delivery
  area, ball, goalkeeper, and taker.
- `player-registration.ts`, `ball-registration.ts`, and
  `stadium-registration.ts` convert authoritative anchors once. The physical
  ball mesh remains a `0.11 m` radius; a separate UI ring and touch target
  preserve mobile readability and input without enlarging collisions.
- `player-orientation.ts` interpolates only relevant player rotations. The
  Legend alone receives the `YOU` label; a receiving teammate remains a
  teammate and carries the ball in front of the authored attacking direction.
- `trajectory-playback.ts` renders the exact top-level pass and, when present,
  concatenates the backend automatic teammate shot after a short control hold.
  It changes no point or authoritative terminal outcome.
- `src/match/api-v1/contract.ts` fails closed on incomplete, one-point,
  non-zero-start, non-monotonic, mismatched-final, or partially supplied flight
  contracts. Embedded and top-level current field states must be identical.

## Review Remediation

Two independent review passes found and drove these blocking corrections:

1. The physical ball visual used a readability scale instead of its `0.11 m`
   radius. Physical mesh, UI highlight, and touch target are now separate.
2. One-point paths and a terminal point that disagreed with the final flight
   point could be accepted. Both now fail closed.
3. Automatic teammate shots were not played after the incoming pass. The
   client now plays pass, control hold, and shot in order.
4. Canonical kick results could omit all flight fields, paths could begin after
   `t=0`, and duplicate field states could diverge. All three now reject.
5. The initial 2 m browser proof checked only y distance. It now measures the
   rendered x/y segment against the exact defender and checks height, contact
   identity, and outcome.
